A bounded service path

The conversation confirms scope, sponsor, participants and boundaries. It does not begin an undefined consulting engagement.

This work is for a practical leader who needs the organization to save time or improve consistency without surrendering accuracy, privacy, judgment or trust.

Recognizable starting conditions

  • Staff already use AI, but standards and ownership are inconsistent.
  • Leadership wants visible progress without a clear use case.
  • Impressive demonstrations have not become routine, reviewed work.
  • Privacy, accuracy or reputational concerns are slowing experimentation.
  • A real communication or operational task creates enough repetition to justify a pilot.

What the engagement may establish

  • A prioritized set of real use cases and explicit decisions about what not to automate.
  • Defined human and AI responsibilities.
  • One or more bounded, tested workflows.
  • Review and quality standards tied to the consequence.
  • Staff guidance based on actual tasks rather than generic AI training.
  • Reusable working resources that reduce future dependency.

Participation required

Strong fit requires an executive sponsor or authorized decision-maker, access to the people performing the work and willingness to pilot narrowly. The engagement cannot repair a workflow if leadership will not support changes to intake, ownership, priorities or review.

This service is not:

  • AI evangelism or adopt-now pressure.
  • A tool-shopping exercise.
  • Generic prompt training disconnected from real work.
  • A promise to remove human review.
  • Enterprise cybersecurity, legal or regulatory compliance advice.
  • A headcount-reduction program disguised as workflow improvement.
Completion should be observable. The team should leave with tested work, documented responsibilities and clearer decisions—not a strategy deck full of possible future uses.
