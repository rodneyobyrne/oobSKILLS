# AI Workday Review - Product Specification

**Version:** 1.0  
**Release model:** Free browser-based assessment  
**Primary URL:** `/assessments/ai-workday-review/`  
**Primary user:** An owner, operations lead, communications lead or practical AI adopter who can name a recurring responsibility but does not yet have a defensible first AI workflow.

## Product job

Replace ambient pressure to "do something with AI" with one bounded decision:

- pilot now;
- prepare the conditions first;
- test a smaller support task; or
- keep the consequential decision human.

The result must reduce choices, protect human responsibility and remain useful when specific AI vendors change.

## Emotional alignment

| Recognizable condition | Protected value | Product movement |
|---|---|---|
| Alert caution about unsafe or unjustified AI use | Trust, responsibility and accuracy | Urgency to informed confidence |
| Founder or leader bottleneck | Quality and judgment | Controlled pressure to evidence for release |
| Reactive communication work | Professional agency | Irritation to a bounded, governable task |
| Lean mission-driven team | Mission and people served | Personal burden to shared responsibility |

The assessment never frames hesitation as failure, exaggerates the cost of waiting or produces a frightening score to force a sale.

## Experience

1. **What matters:** role, pressure and protected value.
2. **The recurring work:** work areas, bounded responsibility, frequency, effort and current friction.
3. **Risk and readiness:** judgment, consequence, sensitivity, source quality, reviewer and reversibility.
4. **A workable pilot:** accountable owner, measure, review capacity, quality standard and stopping condition.
5. **WORKFILE result:** readiness decision, opportunity map, 14-day pilot, boundaries and three reusable working briefs.

The form saves a local draft on the current device. It does not send answers to a server or model. The user can clear the draft at any time.

## Deterministic decision model

The result combines a readiness score with non-negotiable blockers. The public experience does not display a numeric score because the decision and its reasons are more useful than gamification.

### Positive readiness conditions

- repeated task and enough effort to justify testing;
- stable or moderate judgment requirements;
- low consequence that can be detected before use;
- public or approved ordinary internal information;
- current and identifiable source material;
- named reviewer with time and authority;
- reversible test; and
- sufficient review capacity.

### Blocking or preparation conditions

- high-consequence health, safety, rights, financial or professional-advice outcome;
- regulated or highly sensitive information;
- no reliable reviewer;
- no practical way to stop or reverse the change;
- unreliable source information; or
- review capacity too weak to evaluate the test.

### Decision order

1. High consequence or regulated information -> **Keep the decision human.** Offer only a smaller, de-identified, low-consequence support step.
2. Any unresolved blocker or low readiness -> **Prepare first.** Name the specific condition that must change.
3. High human-judgment requirement without a blocker -> **Pilot a smaller support task.** Keep interpretation, promises and approval human.
4. Sufficient readiness without blockers -> **Pilot now - narrowly.** Test one reversible support task for 14 days.

## Output contract

Every completed result includes:

- a plain-language readiness decision and explanation;
- the pressure to reduce and value to protect;
- up to three opportunity areas selected by the user;
- blocker or preparation conditions;
- one bounded 14-day pilot;
- named human responsibility;
- permitted and prohibited input guidance;
- quality standard, measure and stopping condition;
- preparation, review and handoff working briefs;
- browser print/PDF support; and
- downloadable Markdown.

The output must never:

- authorize an AI vendor or sensitive-data use;
- provide legal, cybersecurity, medical, financial or compliance advice;
- imply that generic "human review" is sufficient without a named owner;
- recommend automating high-consequence decisions; or
- convert intended outcomes into past proof.

## Free and paid boundary

The foundational product is free because a responsible first AI-use decision should not require buying a shallow score or expiring prompt bundle.

A future paid organization edition must add capabilities such as:

- multiple participant perspectives;
- private server-side processing;
- organization-specific policy and approval rules;
- durable history and shared review;
- managed implementation; or
- a human decision session.

Do not charge for the current browser result merely by adding a payment gate.

## Maintenance

- Review decision rules quarterly and after any material privacy or platform change.
- Test at least one scenario for each decision outcome after code changes.
- Keep all result language vendor-neutral.
- Record any real support question that indicates confusing boundaries or a missing decision path.
- Do not add new product branches until completion patterns show a distinct user job.

## Required QA scenarios

1. **Pilot now:** frequent, low-consequence, approved-source drafting with a named reviewer and reversible test.
2. **Prepare first:** useful task with scattered sources or no reliable review time.
3. **Smaller support task:** expert judgment is central, but low-consequence preparation can be isolated.
4. **Keep human:** regulated data or a high-consequence decision.
5. **Privacy:** no answer is transmitted through application code; local draft can be cleared.
6. **Accessibility:** every control has a visible label, keyboard order is logical, invalid fields are identified and generated results receive focus.
7. **Portability:** Markdown download, clipboard copy and print/PDF work with user-provided text safely escaped in the rendered result.
