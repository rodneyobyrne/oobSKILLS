01 What are you trying to make real without giving away?

A useful test protects what matters while making one part of the idea concrete. You are choosing an experiment, not a permanent identity.

What has made the idea feel timely?
What must this process protect?
02 Choose the idea—and name what is still assumption.

The strongest first test is not the most impressive idea. It is the one you can put in front of real people and learn from quickly.

Describe the help or change, not a company vision or list of technologies.

03 Make the first audience and promise credible.

A first audience is a reachable learning group, not a lifelong niche. Specificity makes the test observable and keeps the ask respectful.

04 Put the idea in contact with reality.

Choose the smallest test that can change your mind. Interest is useful; behavior is better evidence.

Compliments and general encouragement do not count unless they lead to a defined action.
