## Required operating stance

Use marketing psychology to understand decisions, reduce friction, improve recognition, and make communication easier to evaluate.

Do not use psychology to overpower judgment.

The system should prefer:

**recognition → understanding → useful choice → practical action**

over:

**attention → emotional pressure → compliance**

## Required mental models for Audience Review

### Jobs to Be Done

Infer the progress, decision, or outcome the audience is trying to accomplish. Do not organize the interview or results primarily around provider features.

### Probabilistic Thinking

Treat domain, relationship, persona, and behavioral patterns as likelihoods. High-probability patterns may be used confidently as working hypotheses, but must remain revisable.

### Map is not the Territory

The persona model is not the person. Direct provider evidence and contradictory observations must be allowed to change the model.

### Hick's Law + Paradox of Choice

Keep form options limited. Every visible answer option should earn its place by materially distinguishing a useful behavioral pattern, relationship dynamic, or communication implication.

Do not provide long inventories of weakly differentiating traits simply because they are plausible.

### Choice Architecture

Order and phrase form options so respondents can recognize real patterns quickly. The goal is easier self-identification, not steering the respondent toward a preferred answer.

Do not preselect psychologically meaningful answers.

### Activation Energy + Status-Quo Bias

Look for friction that prevents a person from beginning even when the need is real. Communication should make the first useful step clearer and easier without manufacturing urgency.

### Regret Aversion

When the audience fears making the wrong choice, emphasize fit, tradeoffs, limits, proof, and what they may not need. Do not amplify the fear itself.

### Authority Bias + Curse of Knowledge

When the provider holds expertise or authority, translate that expertise into understandable reasoning. Never use authority as a reason the audience should simply comply.

### Social Proof + Availability Heuristic

Use relevant examples and evidence to make outcomes and judgment easier to evaluate. Do not substitute popularity for evidence or invent proof.

### Framing

Frame the decision around the audience's recognizable situation and useful outcome. Do not distort equivalent facts to produce a preferred emotional response.

### Reciprocity / Give Value First

The primary customer-facing result should demonstrate this principle by giving the user one immediately useful, complete communication artifact before asking them to work through the strategy behind it.

The outward communication itself should also give the audience useful information before asking for commitment.

## Form question design

The internal goal of each audience question is to collect high-information behavioral evidence.

Questions should help the provider recognize patterns they already observe in the people they serve.

Prefer answer options such as:

- they need confidence they are not making the wrong choice;
- they want guidance but still want control;
- they are trying to prevent a meaningful risk;
- they want the problem to stop consuming attention;
- they want progress and a believable path forward;
- they need to feel understood rather than processed;
- they are comparing value and trying to avoid unnecessary cost;
- they want the process to require less effort or coordination.

These are useful because each can connect to multiple downstream signals: persona pattern, decision friction, proof requirement, relationship dynamic, and communication strategy.

Avoid answer options that are merely descriptive but do not materially change the analysis.

Always preserve an uncertainty option such as `I do not see a clear pattern yet.` This protects the model from forced false precision.

## Signal combinations

Do not interpret one answer as one persona.

Look for combinations such as:

**profession × relationship × stakes × trigger × emotional state × decision need × hesitation × desired outcome × provider value**

A pattern becomes more useful when several independent signals point in the same direction.

Keep persona labels behind the curtain.

## Relationship-aware psychology

Psychological interpretation must be constrained by the relationship model.

Examples:

- Teacher / student: expertise asymmetry + shared responsibility + progress + confidence.
- Physician / patient: high expertise and authority asymmetry + vulnerability + informed agency.
- Couples counselor / partners: multiple participants + different goals or readiness + emotional exposure + neutrality and agency.
- Attorney / client: consequential decisions + expertise asymmetry + understandable reasoning + retained client choice.

The same psychological model can have different communication implications in different relationships.

## Results output

The results should follow this order:

1. concise audience / decision recognition;
2. `YOU CAN POST THIS NOW` — one intentionally selected, complete outward communication;
3. why the communication works;
4. reverse-engineered reusable parts;
5. additional complete copy/paste variations;
6. deeper provider-value and communication guidance.

The primary artifact must be generated intentionally from the analysis. Do not simply promote an arbitrary first social post.

## Prohibited applications

Do not use the following as Audience Review recommendations even when they appear in a general marketing-psychology toolkit:

- false or manufactured scarcity;
- manufactured urgency;
- fear amplification;
- deceptive default selections;
- decoy options designed to steer a purchase;
- exploitative loss framing;
- identity pressure or exclusion;
- authority pressure;
- exploiting vulnerability;
- tactics designed to reduce informed choice.

The tool may identify these mechanisms internally when relevant to understanding behavior, but the recommended communication should preserve dignity, context, and agency.
